1. The 1D Scalar Topology Bottleneck

In standard procedural gradient engines (such as Simplex Noise or 1D Domain Warping), pixel colors are derived by projecting a scalar field $t(x, y) \in [0, 1]$ onto a 1D spline sequence of palette colors $[C_0, C_1, C_2, C_3]$. This imposes a rigid topological constraint: color $C_1$ can only ever touch $C_0$ and $C_2$. It is topologically impossible for $C_1$ and $C_3$ to meet without forcing a crossing through $C_2$, resulting in repetitive concentric contour bands across the canvas.

1D Scalar Gradient (Domain Warp)
Strict 1D Manifold

Colors are bound to the sequential order: Deep Black → Crimson → Cyan → Gold. Every transition to Gold must cross through Cyan. Accents cannot exist as independent celestial or atmospheric emitters.

2D Spatial Field (Radial Basis Function)
Independent 2D Emitters

Colors are distributed as independent 2D spatial emitters anchored against an ambient background foundation. Crimson, Cyan, and Gold emerge and interact freely across canvas coordinates without forced intermediary transitions.
